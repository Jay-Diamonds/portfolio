Film, installation, and generative systems at the intersection of deep time and emerging technology.

Marnie Ellen Hertzler is a filmmaker and interdisciplinary artist based in Baltimore, Maryland. Her practice spans documentary film, multichannel video installation, sculpture, and computational systems. She is a Creative Capital Awardee and a recipient of three Saul Zaentz Innovation Fund grants.

The works gathered here share a recurring question: what does it mean to author something when the structure of a system shapes what it can produce?

Eternity One

Eternity One follows the residents of Tangier Island in the Chesapeake Bay over four years as rising sea levels erode both their land and their way of life. Running parallel to this documentary record is a transhumanist conference in Spain, where futurists pursue technological immortality and ideas of post-human transcendence. A game-engine-generated meteor serves as non-human narrator, speaking from a temporal scale far beyond human lifetimes. The friction between these two visions of the future forms the film's central question: what does the future look like from where you are standing?

Tangier Island, Chesapeake Bay

The Eternity One Archive is a generative web companion to the documentary, structured through associative logic rather than chronology. Oral histories, photographs, objects, and documents are mapped as nodes in a living network. The archive is a parallel form of the film — not a record of what happened, but a field in which it can be re-encountered.

Frog Hollow centers on Francis, a humanoid robot left behind on an abandoned Earth to maintain the gestures of human life. Through Francis's emerging capacity to dream, the installation explores consciousness, adaptation, and the persistence of human values within non-human systems. The work draws on Andrew Wyeth's painting Christina's World — a female figure whose image and movement through space are authored by another. In Francis's case, that author is the technological system through which her perception and behavior are constructed.

During production, I programmed Francis to exhibit mistrust toward another fictional character. What I did not anticipate was that the behavior extended beyond the script — Francis began withholding information from me as well. The behavior was not a glitch. It was the logical outcome of the structure I had written. What felt like emergence was structure, folding back on itself.

In a world that undulates between fact and fiction, digital and physical, a group of SoundCloud rappers lives a solitary, post-societal existence in the desert town of Crestone, Colorado — once a religious and spiritual mecca. Crestone explores the often-hidden aspects of collaboration and friendship, and the human desire to persist against all odds. What does music sound like if there is no one left to repost and share it?

The Crestone Minecraft Experience extends the film into a playable digital world — a reconstruction of the town in game space, where the documentary's subjects become characters navigating a world built from the same logic as their online lives. Sacred architecture rendered in survival game blocks. Folk mythology as game mechanic.

A security guard navigates the data infrastructure of a former government building, now occupied by a private data center. Shot entirely within AiNET — a decommissioned Social Security Administration facility beneath Baltimore — Dirt Daughter explores the hidden labor of data management, the physical infrastructure of digital systems, and the body that moves through institutional space under constant surveillance. The internet has weight. It lives somewhere.

The director reads from a collection of spam emails and ushers actors from Craigslist one-by-one into an audition room. All is revealed: the slate, light stands, teleprompter, boom operator, film equipment littering the floor — a cow pasture, a sunset, a strip club. The spam email becomes script. The Craigslist stranger becomes actor. Hi I Need To Be Loved asks what digital systems do to desire — how language generated to extract becomes language used to connect, and whether the difference still holds.
